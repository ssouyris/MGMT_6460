The Essence of Duality

Every LP Has a Twin

Primal and dual

Every linear program has a partner LP called its dual. The original problem is the primal. Both use the same numbers, in different places.

  • A maximization primal has a minimization dual
  • Solving one solves the other
  • The dual’s variables are prices for the primal’s resources

Why managers care

The dual answers a question the primal leaves open: what is one more unit of each resource worth?

The Pair in Standard Form

Primal

\[ \begin{aligned} \max~ Z &= \sum_{j=1}^{n} c_j x_j \\ \text{s.t.} \quad \sum_{j=1}^{n} a_{ij} x_j &\le b_i, \quad i = 1, \dots, m \\ x_j &\ge 0, \quad j = 1, \dots, n \end{aligned} \]

Dual

\[ \begin{aligned} \min~ W &= \sum_{i=1}^{m} b_i y_i \\ \text{s.t.} \quad \sum_{i=1}^{m} a_{ij} y_i &\ge c_j, \quad j = 1, \dots, n \\ y_i &\ge 0, \quad i = 1, \dots, m \end{aligned} \]

  • One dual variable \(y_i\) per primal constraint
  • One dual constraint per primal variable \(x_j\)

Three Swaps Build the Dual

Primal to dual, for a primal in standard form
In the primal Becomes in the dual
Objective coefficients \(c_j\) Right-hand sides
Right-hand sides \(b_i\) Objective coefficients
Column \(j\) of \(A\) (coefficients of \(x_j\)) Row \(j\) of the dual constraints
Maximize, \(\le\) constraints Minimize, \(\ge\) constraints

One rule behind all three

The data matrix is transposed. Rows of the primal become columns of the dual, and the vectors \(b\) and \(c\) trade places.

Wyndor: Primal and Dual

Primal (lecture 01)

\[ \begin{aligned} \max~ Z = 3x_1 &+ 5x_2 \\ \text{s.t.} \quad x_1 \phantom{{}+2x_2} &\le 4 \\ 2x_2 &\le 12 \\ 3x_1 + 2x_2 &\le 18 \\ x_1, x_2 &\ge 0 \end{aligned} \]

Dual

\[ \begin{aligned} \min~ W = 4y_1 + 12y_2 &+ 18y_3 \\ \text{s.t.} \quad y_1 \phantom{{}+2y_2} + 3y_3 &\ge 3 \\ 2y_2 + 2y_3 &\ge 5 \\ y_1, y_2, y_3 &\ge 0 \end{aligned} \]

  • Three plants give three dual variables: \(y_i\) belongs to Plant \(i\)
  • Two products give two dual constraints

Wyndor: Each Constraint Has a Partner

Primal (lecture 01)

\[ \begin{aligned} \max~ Z = 3x_1 &+ 5x_2 \\ \text{s.t.} \quad x_1 \phantom{{}+2x_2} &\le 4 && \textcolor{#D6001C}{(y_1)} \\ 2x_2 &\le 12 && \textcolor{#D6001C}{(y_2)} \\ 3x_1 + 2x_2 &\le 18 && \textcolor{#D6001C}{(y_3)} \\ x_1, x_2 &\ge 0 \end{aligned} \]

Dual

\[ \begin{aligned} \min~ W = 4y_1 + 12y_2 &+ 18y_3 \\ \text{s.t.} \quad y_1 \phantom{{}+2y_2} + 3y_3 &\ge 3 && \textcolor{#D6001C}{(x_1)} \\ 2y_2 + 2y_3 &\ge 5 && \textcolor{#D6001C}{(x_2)} \\ y_1, y_2, y_3 &\ge 0 \end{aligned} \]

  • Primal constraint \(i\) pairs with dual variable \(y_i\)
  • Dual constraint \(j\) pairs with primal variable \(x_j\)

Wyndor in Matrix Form

Wyndor data

\[ \mathbf{c} = \begin{bmatrix} 3 & 5 \end{bmatrix}, \qquad \mathbf{x} = \begin{bmatrix} x_1 \\ x_2 \end{bmatrix} \]

\[ \mathbf{A} = \begin{bmatrix} 1 & 0 \\ 0 & 2 \\ 3 & 2 \end{bmatrix}, \qquad \mathbf{b} = \begin{bmatrix} 4 \\ 12 \\ 18 \end{bmatrix} \]

\[ \mathbf{y} = \begin{bmatrix} y_1 & y_2 & y_3 \end{bmatrix} \]

Primal

\[ \max~ Z = \mathbf{cx} \quad \text{s.t.} \quad \mathbf{Ax} \le \mathbf{b}, \;\; \mathbf{x} \ge \mathbf{0} \]

Dual

\[ \min~ W = \mathbf{yb} \quad \text{s.t.} \quad \mathbf{yA} \ge \mathbf{c}, \;\; \mathbf{y} \ge \mathbf{0} \]

  • \(\mathbf{x}\) is a column vector; \(\mathbf{y}\) is a row vector
  • This notation is used from here on

One Table, Two Problems

Primal-dual table for Wyndor
\(x_1\) \(x_2\)
\(y_1\) 1 0 \(\le 4\)
\(y_2\) 0 2 \(\le 12\)
\(y_3\) 3 2 \(\le 18\)
\(\ge 3\) \(\ge 5\)
  • Across a row: a primal constraint
  • Down a column: a dual constraint
  • Right column: dual objective
  • Bottom row: primal objective

Symmetry

Each constraint in one problem is a variable’s column in the other.

What the Dual Prices Mean

The Dual as a Pricing Problem

Economic reading of the Wyndor dual

\(y_i\) is the value, in $ thousands, of one hour of production time at Plant \(i\).

  • Objective. Minimize the value of all hours: \(W = 4y_1 + 12y_2 + 18y_3\)
  • Doors. Their hours must be worth $3,000 or more: \(y_1 + 3y_3 \ge 3\)
  • Windows. Their hours must be worth $5,000 or more: \(2y_2 + 2y_3 \ge 5\)

The idea

The lowest prices at which every product’s hours cover its profit.

Wyndor’s Dual Prices

Dual optimum: the shadow prices

\(y_1^* = 0, \quad y_2^* = 1.5, \quad y_3^* = 1, \qquad W^* = 4(0) + 12(1.5) + 18(1) = 36 = Z^*\)

Plant Hours used Available Idle Shadow price
Plant 1 2 4 2 $0
Plant 2 12 12 0 $1,500 / h
Plant 3 18 18 0 $1,000 / h
  • Plant 1 has idle hours: one more is worth $0
  • One more hour at Plant 2 adds $1,500

A Shadow Price Is a Slope

\(Z^*\) is piecewise linear in each \(b_i\). Its slope at today’s capacity is \(y_i^*\). The slope holds only over a range: 6 to 18 hours at Plant 2.

The Duality Properties

Weak Duality

Weak duality property

If \(\mathbf{x}\) is feasible for the primal and \(\mathbf{y}\) is feasible for the dual, then \(\mathbf{cx} \le \mathbf{yb}\).

Proof in one line. Use \(\mathbf{yA} \ge \mathbf{c}\) with \(\mathbf{x} \ge \mathbf{0}\), then \(\mathbf{Ax} \le \mathbf{b}\) with \(\mathbf{y} \ge \mathbf{0}\):

\[ \mathbf{cx} \;\le\; (\mathbf{yA})\mathbf{x} \;=\; \mathbf{y}(\mathbf{Ax}) \;\le\; \mathbf{yb}. \]

Why it is useful

Any dual feasible \(\mathbf{y}\) gives an upper bound on the best profit. Wyndor: \(\mathbf{y} = (3, 2.5, 0)\) is dual feasible, so no plan earns more than \(W = 42\).

Weak Duality in Pictures

Every primal value sits left of 36. Every dual value sits right of it. The two sets touch at the optimum and nowhere else.

Strong Duality

Strong duality property

If \(\mathbf{x}^*\) is optimal for the primal and \(\mathbf{y}^*\) is optimal for the dual, then \(\mathbf{cx}^* = \mathbf{y}^*\mathbf{b}\).

  • Wyndor: \(Z^* = 36\) at \((2, 6)\) and \(W^* = 36\) at \((0, 1.5, 1)\)
  • The gap between the two problems closes to zero

A certificate of optimality

Given a feasible \(\mathbf{x}\) and a feasible \(\mathbf{y}\) with \(\mathbf{cx} = \mathbf{yb}\), both are optimal. Weak duality says no plan can beat \(\mathbf{yb}\), and \(\mathbf{x}\) reaches it.

Practice: Check a Proposed Plan

Practice Problem

A consultant claims \((x_1, x_2) = (2, 6)\) is optimal for Wyndor. Verify the claim with \(\mathbf{y} = (0, 1.5, 1)\) and no simplex iterations.

  1. Is \((2, 6)\) primal feasible?
  2. Is \((0, 1.5, 1)\) dual feasible?
  3. Compare \(Z\) and \(W\). What do you conclude?

Check

  1. \(2 \le 4\), \(12 \le 12\), \(18 \le 18\). (b) \(0 + 3 = 3 \ge 3\) and \(3 + 2 = 5 \ge 5\). (c) \(Z = W = 36\), so both are optimal.

The Duality Theorem

Only three pairings of outcomes can occur.

Possible outcomes for a primal-dual pair
Dual has an optimum Dual is unbounded Dual is infeasible
Primal has an optimum Possible, \(Z^* = W^*\) Never Never
Primal is unbounded Never Never Possible
Primal is infeasible Never Possible Possible
  • Feasible and bounded on one side: the same on the other side
  • Unbounded on one side: the other side has no feasible solution
  • Infeasible on one side: the other is unbounded or infeasible

Symmetry: The Dual of the Dual Is the Primal

\[ \underbrace{\min\, \mathbf{yb},\;\; \mathbf{yA} \ge \mathbf{c}}_{\text{dual}} \;\longrightarrow\; \underbrace{\max\, -\mathbf{yb},\;\; -\mathbf{yA} \le -\mathbf{c}}_{\text{standard form}} \;\longrightarrow\; \underbrace{\min\, -\mathbf{cx},\;\; -\mathbf{Ax} \ge -\mathbf{b}}_{\text{its dual}} \;\longrightarrow\; \underbrace{\max\, \mathbf{cx},\;\; \mathbf{Ax} \le \mathbf{b}}_{\text{the primal}} \]

with \(\mathbf{y} \ge \mathbf{0}\) and \(\mathbf{x} \ge \mathbf{0}\) throughout.

Symmetry property

Every relationship between a primal and its dual holds in both directions. Which problem you call “primal” is a label.

Why Duality Is Useful

  1. Solve the easier problem. Simplex effort grows mainly with the number of constraints. With many more constraints than variables, solve the dual.
  2. Certify a plan. A dual feasible \(\mathbf{y}\) with the same objective proves optimality.
  3. Price resources. Shadow prices value capacity, labor and budget.
  4. Run sensitivity analysis. A change in the primal becomes a simple check in the dual (last section).

Primal-Dual Relationships

The Dual in Augmented Form

Subtract a surplus variable, written \(z_j - c_j\), from each dual constraint \(j\).

\[ \begin{aligned} y_1 + 3y_3 - (z_1 - c_1) &= 3 \\ 2y_2 + 2y_3 - (z_2 - c_2) &= 5 \end{aligned} \]

  • \(n\) constraints and \(n + m\) variables: Wyndor has 2 and 5
  • A basic solution has \(n\) basic and \(m\) nonbasic variables

Read the dual from row 0

At any simplex iteration, row 0 of the primal tableau holds a dual solution. The coefficient of slack \(x_{n+i}\) is \(y_i\). The coefficient of \(x_j\) is \(z_j - c_j\).

Complementary slackness property

In complementary basic solutions, each pair has one basic and one nonbasic variable. So at least one of the pair equals zero. The primal has \(m\) basic variables; the dual has \(n\).

Adapting to Other Primal Forms

Any LP Has a Dual

  • Option 1. Convert the model to standard form, then build the dual as usual
  • Option 2. Build the dual directly with the SOB method

Symmetry again

The dual of the dual is the primal. So it does not matter which problem is labeled primal.

The SOB Method

Label every constraint and every sign restriction Sensible, Odd or Bizarre.

Corresponding primal-dual forms
Label Maximization problem Minimization problem
Constraint \(i\) Variable \(y_i\)
Sensible \(\le\) \(y_i \ge 0\)
Odd \(=\) \(y_i\) unconstrained
Bizarre \(\ge\) \(y_i \le 0\)
Variable \(x_j\) Constraint \(j\)
Sensible \(x_j \ge 0\) \(\ge\)
Odd \(x_j\) unconstrained \(=\)
Bizarre \(x_j \le 0\) \(\le\)

SOB in Three Steps

  1. Write the primal as a max or a min. The dual takes the other.
  2. Constraint \(\to\) dual variable. Give \(y_i\) the label of primal constraint \(i\).
  3. Variable \(\to\) dual constraint. Give dual constraint \(j\) the label of the sign restriction on \(x_j\).

Memory aid

“Sensible” is the form a textbook model would use. In a max, \(\le\) capacity limits. In a min, \(\ge\) requirements. Nonnegative variables in both.

Example: The Radiation Therapy Dual

Primal (lecture 01), a min

\[ \begin{aligned} \min~ Z = 0.4x_1 &+ 0.5x_2 \\ \text{s.t.} \quad 0.3x_1 + 0.1x_2 &\le 2.7 \quad \text{B} \\ 0.5x_1 + 0.5x_2 &= 6 \quad\;\;\, \text{O} \\ 0.6x_1 + 0.4x_2 &\ge 6 \quad\;\;\, \text{S} \\ x_1, x_2 &\ge 0 \quad\;\;\, \text{S} \end{aligned} \]

Dual, a max

\[ \begin{aligned} \max~ W = 2.7y_1 + 6y_2 &+ 6y_3 \\ \text{s.t.} \quad 0.3y_1 + 0.5y_2 + 0.6y_3 &\le 0.4 \\ 0.1y_1 + 0.5y_2 + 0.4y_3 &\le 0.5 \\ y_1 \le 0, \;\; y_2 \text{ free}, \;\; y_3 &\ge 0 \end{aligned} \]

  • Constraints B, O, S give \(y_1 \le 0\), \(y_2\) free, \(y_3 \ge 0\)
  • \(x_1, x_2 \ge 0\) are S, so both dual constraints are \(\le\)

What the Therapy Dual Says

Dual optimum

\(y^* = (-0.5,\; 1.1,\; 0)\) and \(W^* = 2.7(-0.5) + 6(1.1) = 5.25 = Z^*\).

  • \(y_1^* = -0.5\). Relax the critical-tissue limit by 0.1 kilorad and the healthy-tissue dose falls by 0.05
  • \(y_2^* = 1.1\). Each extra kilorad prescribed for the tumor costs 1.1 kilorads to healthy tissue
  • \(y_3^* = 0\). The tumor-center requirement is slack at the optimum: \(0.6(7.5) + 0.4(4.5) = 6.3 > 6\)

Signs make sense

In a min, loosening a \(\le\) limit can only lower cost. So its dual price is \(\le 0\): exactly what SOB predicted.

Practice: Write a Dual with SOB

Practice Problem

Write the dual of

\[ \begin{aligned} \max~ Z = 4x_1 &+ 3x_2 \\ \text{s.t.} \quad x_1 + 2x_2 &\le 10 \\ 2x_1 + x_2 &\ge 4 \\ x_1 - x_2 &= 1 \\ x_1 \ge 0, \;\; &x_2 \text{ unconstrained} \end{aligned} \]

Duality in Sensitivity Analysis

Primal or Dual: Use the Easier One

  • Sensitivity analysis asks how the optimum responds to changes in the data
  • Some changes are easier to check in the dual
  • A new variable in the primal is a new constraint in the dual
  • A change to a nonbasic variable’s coefficients cannot make the current solution infeasible. It can make it suboptimal

The one-line test

The current solution stays optimal if and only if the complementary dual solution stays feasible.

Pricing Out a New Product

Wyndor considers Product 3: $4,000 per batch, using 0, 1 and 2 hours at Plants 1, 2, 3.

New dual constraint. \(\;0\,y_1 + 1\,y_2 + 2\,y_3 \ge 4\)

Check at \(\mathbf{y}^* = (0, 1.5, 1)\). \(\;0 + 1.5 + 2 = 3.5 < 4\). Violated.

Reading

At current shadow prices the hours cost $3,500 and earn $4,000. Product 3 is worth making, so \((2, 6)\) is no longer optimal. Re-solving gives \(Z^* = 39\) with \((x_1, x_2, x_3) = (0, 3, 6)\).

  3. What is the lowest profit that makes it worth adding?

Check

  1. \(y_1 + y_2 + 3y_3 \ge c_4\). (b) The hours are worth \(0 + 1.5 + 3 = 4.5\), so $4,000: no; $6,000: yes. (c) Anything above $4,500.

Conclusions

Conclusions

  • Every LP has a dual, built by transposing the data
  • Weak duality gives bounds. Strong duality makes the two optima equal
  • Dual variables are shadow prices: the value of one more unit of a resource, over a stated range
  • Complementary slackness: idle resources are free, and products made earn exactly the value of what they use
  • SOB builds the dual of any LP directly
  • A new product is checked with one dual constraint

Summary

Core takeaways

  • \(\mathbf{cx} \le \mathbf{yb}\) for any feasible pair
  • \(Z^* = W^*\) at the optimum
  • \(y_i^*\) = slope of \(Z^*\) in \(b_i\)

Watch out for

  • Shadow prices hold only within the allowable range
  • Signs of dual variables in nonstandard forms
  • Nonunique prices at breakpoints

Bottom line

The primal says what to do. The dual says what each resource is worth.
